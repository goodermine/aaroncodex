# Aaron — Song-Fit Reference

**What sits easy in your voice — your 47 songs, ranked from your own takes.**

> Each song is placed by how *you* actually sing it (your key, your recorded take), not the
> original artist. All figures are **raw measures** (always comparable — no scores).
> **†** = one take on file, so provisional.

> **Revised 9 Aug 2026 — the ranking model changed.** The first version of this
> sheet assumed *low = easy, high = hard*. Measured evidence says that's only
> half right. Your **strongest zone is M2/mixed — core tessitura A3–B4 with the
> gear change around D4** — and your best-measured songs all live there. Songs
> that sit **lower** (E3–G4) aren't easier; they force constant register
> crossings, trigger the "Pitch Slider" scooping pattern, and measure *worse*.
> So a song is now placed on **where it sits relative to A3–B4**, how much
> register-crossing it forces, and breath-sag/drift as tie-breakers — not on
> height alone. See `memory/016-m2-mixed-strongest-zone.md`.

> **The thing worth knowing about your own ears:** you've said the M2/mixed
> sound doesn't feel great to you — "I'm not used to my M2 mixed voice." That's
> unfamiliarity, not inaccuracy. It is measurably where you are *most precise*.
> Trust the zone before you trust the feeling.

## Hard — three different reasons

Sit **too high**, sit **too low and force register crossings**, or tax your
**breath/steadiness**. The middle one is new in this revision — and it's the
reason a low song can be harder for you than a high one.
