# Aaron — The Improvement Brief

**The reading version.** Everything here is measured from your own takes —
100+ recordings scored note-by-note against 50 professional reference vocals.
No opinion, no guesswork.

> **The whole brief in four sentences.**
> Your landings are already professional — now clean up the *approach*: see it, land it, stay on it.
> Live lighter on the gear change you stand on all night.
> Keep the ribs wide to the last word, and keep held notes still before you shape them.
> When the lights are on: one gear in reserve — the microphone does the loudness.

---

## 1. Where you actually are

You are measurably good — not "good for karaoke", good against professionals.
The instrument is already there; what's left is aim.

| Strength | Measured | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Landing accuracy | 20–25 cents from centre | Pro-level, and stable since 2019 — **don't spend practice on it** |
| Range | A2 – E5 (~2.5 octaves) | Full functional range; falsetto ceiling E5 |
| "Money zone" | B3 – B4 | Where your voice reads genuinely world-class |
| Under pressure | **9.3 capture-fair** at Brighton | Sung *blind* — backing so loud you couldn't hear yourself |

*(Capture-fair scale: 10 = a typical professional. That 9.3 was a live take with zero self-monitoring.)*

Everything below sits on top of that. There are only three things to fix:
the way you **start** notes, the way you **hold** them, and the way you
**finish** phrases.

## 2. The three gaps

| Gap | You | Reference | The verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Approach** — clean note entries | 23.7% (16th percentile) | Pro median 33.2%; Farnham 57.1% | **Biggest gap.** Your landing is fine; your approach isn't |
| **Hold** — drift inside a held note | 39.8 cents | Farnham 15 cents | What listeners feel most |
| **Finish** — phrase endings that sag | 51.5% | Farnham 28.6–29.7% | Support released early, not lack of air |

### 2a. Approach — the single highest-value fix

Only ~1 entry in 4 starts clean on the note. Out of 50 pro references, **42
start their notes more cleanly** than your typical take. What happens instead:
you slide *up* into ~48% of notes from nearly a semitone below, and *overshoot*
the top on ~29%. The ear hears all of that as loose pitch — even though the
note you finally sit on is dead centre. The engine scores where you land; the
room hears how you got there.

> **Scooping isn't the flaw.** Farnham scoops from nearly the same depth you do —
> he just does it **3× less often, by choice**. Make the clean entry your
> default, then put the scoop back where you *want* it. That's style instead of habit.

**Cue: see it, land it, stay on it.** Hear the note in your head before you make
a sound. A voice scoops when it's *searching*; take away the search, take away
the scoop.

### 2b. You live on your passaggio

| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Most-used note | **E4** |
| Working range on stage | ~A3 – F♯4 |
| Gear change (passaggio) | **D4 – E4** |

The note you sing most is the note your voice changes gear on. That's why full
nights feel taxing — it's geography, not weakness. So passaggio work is
**maintenance on your most-used range**, not an advanced extra.

The answer is already in your technique: the engine measured Farnham's original
*Pressure Down* vocal at **~76% light production** — not heavy chest. The man
who owns the song treats those exact notes as *lifted speech*. Your lighter
upper setup is the correct professional answer, confirmed by the master. When
the chorus sits on C♯4/D4/E4 — think mix, think lifted speech, let the mic do
the loudness.

### 2c. The honest breath story

Your median phrase is **under 3 seconds** — nobody runs out of air in 3 seconds.
This is **not** a lung-capacity problem; you let go of the support before the
phrase finishes. Some flagged "sags" are also just deliberate rock fall-offs the
originals make too (Livin' on a Prayer, Let's Stay Together). But two songs are
genuinely your weakest and are parked out of the ladder:

| Song | Sagging endings | vs your own mean (51.6%) |
|---|---|---|
| Bye Bye Love | 75.5% | +23.9 |
| You Sexy Thing | 66.6% | +15.0 |
| Pressure Down | 50.6% | −1.0 (fine) |
| My Babe | 48.5% | −3.1 (fine) |

**The fix — the Farinelli breath (silent, safe any day):** in 4, suspend 4,
out 4. The whole exercise is the suspension — and the classic error is holding
it in the wrong place. **The hold is your ribs staying wide, not your throat
closing.** Test: hum straight out of the suspension; a click/catch/push means
you held it at the glottis — reset and hold it at the ribs.
On stage it compresses to three words: **last word loud.**

### 2d. Held notes that wander

A held note of yours drifts ~40 cents; Farnham's move ~15. Two drills:

- **Straight tone against a drone** — 8–10 s, dead straight, no vibrato, no
  swell. Drift is invisible alone, obvious against a reference pitch.
- **Messa di voce** — soft → full → soft on one breath, pitch unmoving.

**Rule: still first, shape second.** If the straight tone isn't solid today,
don't add the swell today. And it's already working: Kung Fu Fighting drift fell
**41.6 → 31.2 cents in a single night** (Brighton). Pro median is ~24.

## 3. The 20-minute daily programme

5–6 days a week, **not 7** — the rest day is when the adaptation lands.
(15-minute version = blocks 0, 1, 2, 3. **Never cut block 0.**)

| # | Block | Min | Moves this number |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **SOVTE wake-up** (straw/water, glides) | 3 | Prepares everything, costs nothing — *never skip* |
| 1 | Farinelli breath — silent | 3 | Sag 51.5% → under 35% |
| 2 | **Onset planting** (the biggest gap) | 5 | Clean entries 23.7% → 33%+ |
| 3 | Sustain → Messa di voce | 4 | Drift 39.8c → under 25c |
| 4 | Passaggio & range | 3 | Working top; M2 in songs |
| 5 | Song application (one phrase) | 2 | Phrase length 2.7s → 4s+ |

**Block 2 in detail** (biggest deficit): *(a)* one pitch three ways — breathy /
hard / balanced — to build the feel; *(b)* silent-target planting — play a note,
stop it, hear it, land it clean, hold 2s, ×10 pitches; *(c)* deliberate contrast
— scoop a known phrase on purpose, then sing it clean, alternate.

### Weekly rotation

Same 20 minutes; one block gets a double share each day.

| Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Sat | Sun |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Onsets (2) | Sustain (3) | Breath (1) — light day | Onsets (2) | Passaggio (4) | Song / gig (5) | **Off / recovery** |

**Recovery version** (tired/post-gig): blocks 0 + 1 only — 6 minutes, costs the
folds almost nothing, keeps the streak. **Deload every 4th week:** halve
everything — adaptation lands in the easy week.

## 4. The campaign — December

The killer in past years was **not skill — it was the pressure response**
(pushing too hard under nerves). The proof it's beatable: Brighton, no
monitoring, best measured live take, by staying inside yourself.

| Round | Date | Song | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm-ups | Oct 7 & 14 | (dress rehearsals) | Sing semi candidates live |
| Heats 1–4 | Oct 21 – Nov 11 | **Kung Fu Fighting** | 9.3 blind at Brighton; survives bad monitoring |
| Quarter-finals | Nov 18 & 25 | **Pressure Down** | Signature; drilled 9× |
| **Semi-final** | **Dec 2** | Showpiece A (data decides) | Your historical wall |
| Grand final | Dec 9 | Showpiece B | — |

Showpiece decided by data by October: **Reasons** (climbed 7.7 → 8.3 in a week)
vs **You're the Voice** (highest ceiling, untested). Backups: Oh What a Night,
One.

**Two load-bearing rules for the night:**

1. **One cue only on stage** — under adrenaline one survives, three don't:
   *"One gear in reserve — the microphone does the loudness. Let the chorus pull
   the power out of me."*
2. **There is no song two.** Your accuracy climbs across a set (intonation
   6.0 → 7.0 → 10.0 over three songs). In a heat you get one song, cold — so
   **arrive in song-three condition**: full warm-up at home, singing in the car,
   quiet sing-along at the venue. Worth more than any drill.

Friday karaoke now has a job: **pressure inoculation.** Score irrelevant;
executing "one gear in reserve" with adrenaline in your blood is the whole
assignment.

## 5. The rules that override everything

- **Never train through hoarseness.** Clears overnight = fatigue. Still there
  after a full rest day = stop sign.
- **Pain is always a stop.** Singing should feel free.
- **Hoarse a week later, or lost top notes that don't return** → a laryngologist
  who scopes, not wait-and-see.
- **Whisper is worse than quiet speech.** On rest days, speak softly; don't whisper.
- **Don't train two systems to failure at once** — you're mid rib-loading; don't
  also push the voice to symptoms the same week.
- **Fresh beats drilled**, especially inside 9 weeks of the competition.

## 6. How you'll know it's working

Don't judge by feel — record **one take of Pressure Down or Reasons a month**,
same conditions, and read four raw numbers (always comparable, no caveats):

| Number | Today | 3-month target |
|---|---|---|
| Clean entries | 23.7% (16th pct) | **33%+ (50th pct)** |
| Sagging endings | 51.5% | **under 35%** |
| Held-note drift | 39.8 cents | **under 25 cents** |
| Median phrase length | 2.7 s | **4 s+** |

**Don't expect the overall /10 to jump** even when all four improve — the things
you're fixing are weighted lightly in the score, and onsets aren't scored at all.
That's a known limitation of the number, not a failure of the work. **The point
is to sound better to a room — and to a panel on December 2nd — not to move a
number.** The blind listening test already proved your ear tracks these even when
the score doesn't.

*You're closer than you think. Twenty minutes a day, five days a week, one
recorded take a month. Everything else is patience.*
