# Gear — Loop Experience 2 earplugs

**What they are.** Reusable filtered earplugs (the *Tomorrowland × Loop
Experience 2* colourway). Unlike foam plugs, they lower the whole sound level
fairly **evenly across frequencies** rather than just killing the highs — so
music (and your own voice) stays natural, just quieter.

| Spec | Value |
|---|---|
| Attenuation | **17 dB SNR** (12 dB NRR) |
| Filter character | Even/low-colouration — keeps sound "crisp," music-focused |
| Fit | 4 silicone tip sizes (XS–L); seal per ear matters |
| Louder-night option | *Experience 2 Plus* adds the **Loop Mute** insert for extra reduction on demand |

**Owned as of:** August 2026 — delivered, in testing.

---

## Why Aaron bought them — hearing protection

Aaron sings/watches at loud karaoke rooms; the Brighton Hotel measures around
**105 dB** just standing there (see `memory/006-brisbane-venues-timezone.md`).
At 105 dB, safe unprotected exposure is only minutes. Knocking ~15–17 dB off
brings that to roughly **88–90 dB**, where safe exposure is hours — a large cut
in dose (every 3 dB roughly halves the sound energy). This alone justifies them.

> Real-world caveat: the 17 dB is a lab figure with a perfect seal. Work the tip
> sizes until each ear gives a clear "sealed" drop; an uneven seal skews the
> pitch reference between ears.

---

## Do they help the *singing*? — **Tested. No.**

The A/B was run on 4 Aug 2026: *Do Wah Diddy*, same song, same session, plugs in
vs plugs out. The result went the opposite way to the prediction.

| Do Wah Diddy | Median pitch deviation | Clean entries |
|---|---|---|
| Earplugs **in** | 20 cents | 31.1% |
| Earplugs **out** | **10 cents** | **40.3%** |

**Plugs in measured worse on both counts** — twice the pitch deviation and ~9
points fewer clean entries. See `memory/013-earplugs-hurt-pitch.md`.

### Why the prediction was wrong

The reasoning *for* them was the **occlusion effect**: your own voice reaches
your ears partly by bone conduction, normally leaking out the open ear canal;
plug it and that gets trapped, so your voice sits louder against the band. That
part is real — they do make you easier to hear.

What it missed is that hearing yourself *louder* is not the same as hearing
yourself *accurately*. Occlusion boosts the low frequencies of your own voice
and changes its timbre inside your head, so the reference you're matching
against is distorted. Given `memory/012-pitch-matching-struggle.md` — conscious
pitch matching is already your weak point, and you sing most in tune when your
voice "just goes there" — a louder but colour-shifted self-signal appears to
pull you further off, not closer.

### Where that leaves them

- **Keep wearing them for hearing protection.** That was the real reason to buy
  them, the 105 dB exposure is genuinely harmful, and the protection case is
  untouched by this result.
- **Don't treat them as a singing aid**, and don't wear them for takes you
  intend to measure or for competition rounds.
- **Worth one more test before closing the book:** a single plug in the ear
  facing the speakers (protection where the blast is, one ear natural). That
  keeps most of the dose reduction while leaving an undistorted reference — the
  one configuration the A/B didn't cover.
- Relates to `memory/008-headphone-discomfort.md` — closed headphones at the
  stand also hurt your singing. Two different devices, same pattern: anything
  that replaces your natural self-monitoring seems to cost you accuracy.

**Status:** owned and in use for hearing protection. Rejected as a singing aid
on measured evidence (4 Aug 2026).
