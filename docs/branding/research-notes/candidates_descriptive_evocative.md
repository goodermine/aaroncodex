# Candidate brand names (descriptive/suggestive + evocative/real-word) for "VOX Analysis", with conflict screening

Method and confidence. Screening ran on 14 Oct 2026 and used three methods:

1. Web searches. The search tool covers the US only, so trademark-register coverage is thin: no Justia, USPTO or IP Australia record surfaced for any candidate, which is not the same as there being no record.
2. Live RDAP registry lookups for domain registration. For .com these went to `https://rdap.verisign.com/com/v1/domain/<name>.com`. For .app and .ai they went through the `https://rdap.org/domain/<name>.<tld>` bootstrap. HTTP 200 means registered, 404 means not registered, and 429 means rate-limited (unknown).
3. Fetching each registered domain's homepage to see what it is used for.

None of this is legal clearance. Before filing, the candidates need a USPTO, IP Australia and WIPO Global Brand Database search in Nice classes 9, 41 and 42. Overall confidence is medium for domain status and for obvious app or company conflicts, and low for trademark-register status.

## Which real-word or musical-term names suggest measurement + voice + coaching yet are still unclaimed in singing/voice software?

### Takeaway
The best openings are **Vocal Verdict** (every domain free, no conflicts found) and **Appoggio** (Italian for "breath support", no app or company found, .app/.ai free, .com buyable for US$3,795). **Singerprint** is the strongest coined option, but its .com/.app are held by a third party. **Takecard** and **Vocal Assay** are clean but less evocative. Most single Italian musical terms are already used in music or audio software.

### Cited Findings

**1. Vocal Verdict.** Style: descriptive/suggestive compound. Risk: **LOW**.
- Meaning and fit: the name restates the positioning line ("a verdict on top" of measurement) and gives a clear consumer promise. "Verdict" sounds authoritative and test-like, but it may read as judgmental to nervous amateur singers. That tension matters because the brand promise includes "a 7 is a good amateur result, not a failure".
- Conflicts: a search for "Vocal Verdict" found no product, show or organisation of that name. The only nearby use was a metaphor in a Cari Cole Substack essay about singers told they can't sing — [Search results incl. Cari Cole Substack](https://substack.caricole.com/p/to-the-man-who-was-told-he-cant-sing)
- Domains: vocalverdict.com, .app and .ai all returned 404 (unregistered). vocalverdicts.com is also free — [Verisign RDAP](https://rdap.verisign.com/com/v1/domain/vocalverdict.com)
- Nearest neighbour: voxverdict.com was registered in 2023 and hosts "VoxVerdict Awards", which is relevant if a Vox-compound were chosen — [voxverdict.com](https://voxverdict.com)
- Trademark strength: "Vocal" is descriptive. The pair is likely registrable as a composite but would be a moderately weak mark. A logo or stylised form would help.

**2. Appoggio.** Style: evocative Italian bel-canto term. Risk: **LOW–MEDIUM**.
- Meaning: appoggio ("leaning", "support") is the Italian singing term for breath support. One source says "support" probably became the common term for singing breath because of appoggio — [Dummies: phrases used during singing lessons](https://www.dummies.com/article/academics-the-arts/music/voice/phrases-used-during-singing-lessons-179295)
- Fit: the product measures breath support directly (phrase endings sagging flat) and coaches on it. The word suggests the coach who supports you, and voice teachers will see the craft in it. The risks are pronunciation (ah-POD-joh) and spelling for lay karaoke users, plus low recognition outside classical training.
- Conflicts: a search for an Appoggio app, company or music trademark found nothing — [search: Appoggio app singing](https://www.dummies.com/article/phrases-used-during-singing-lessons-179295)
- Domains: appoggio.com is listed for sale on HugeDomains at **US$3,795** — [appoggio.com](http://appoggio.com). appoggio.app and .ai were unregistered (RDAP 404), and useappoggio.com and appoggiovocal.com were free.
- Trademark strength: a foreign word used for vocal software may face a "doctrine of foreign equivalents" descriptiveness argument at the USPTO ("support"), but it is suggestive rather than descriptive of an analysis product. That reasoning is mine, not a cited ruling.

**3. Singerprint.** Style: coined portmanteau (singer + fingerprint). Risk: **LOW–MEDIUM**, driven by domains.
- Meaning and fit: a vocal fingerprint for each performance. It suggests identity, provenance and deterministic results ("same audio, same score"), and it is friendly and memorable.
- Conflicts: searches for "Singerprint app singing", "SINGERPRINT trademark" and "singerprint voice" returned no product or trademark of that exact name. Results covered only "voiceprint" biometrics and other FINGERPRINT marks — [search incl. Justia FINGERPRINT mark](https://trademarks.justia.com/903/43/fingerprint-90343250.html); [voiceprint results](https://www.resemble.ai/resources/voiceprint-recognition-properties)
- Domains: singerprint.com was registered on 18 Apr 2020 and no live site responded. singerprint.app is registered (no site); singerprint.ai is free — [Verisign RDAP](https://rdap.verisign.com/com/v1/domain/singerprint.com)
- Caveat: "voiceprint" carries forensic and biometric connotations, and a 2026 arXiv paper critiques "voiceprint" claims of uniqueness — [arXiv 2608.07980](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.07980). This is mostly irrelevant to a singing product but worth knowing.

**4. Takecard.** Style: descriptive compound (take + scorecard). Risk: **LOW–MEDIUM**.
- Meaning and fit: one scorecard per recorded take, which matches the per-song report and progress tracking. It is plain and less emotive.
- Conflicts: a search found no app of that name — [search results](https://www.capterra.co.uk/software/1110860/Vocards)
- Domains: takecard.com was registered in 2014 and serves a Cloudflare challenge page (owner unknown). takecard.app and .ai are free.

**5. Vocal Assay / Sing Assay.** Style: evocative scientific metaphor. Risk: **LOW** for conflicts, **MEDIUM** for comprehension.
- Meaning: an assay is a test of purity or quality (originally of metals). It fits "scientific-grade measurement", and the hallmark connotation fits provenance.
- Conflicts: a search for "Vocal Assay" or "VocalAssay" found nothing — [search results](https://www.capterra.com.sg/software/1110860/Vocards)
- Domains: vocalassay.com and singassay.com are free (RDAP 404).
- Weakness: "assay" is an uncommon word with lab and medical overtones, so it is less singer-friendly.

**6. Tenmark.** Style: suggestive compound ("the 10 mark", where 10 = a typical pro). Risk: **MEDIUM**.
- Fit: it carries the "measure vs pros" calibration idea.
- Conflicts: TenMarks Education was an Amazon-acquired math-practice software company (founded 2009, acquired 2013, apps discontinued 2019). Its name is near-identical in the education/software space, and any surviving registrations would need checking — [Wikipedia: TenMarks Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TenMarks_Education,_Inc.)
- Domains: tenmark.com was registered in 2002 (no site). tenmark.app and .ai are free.

**7. Messa di Voce (or "Messa").** Style: evocative Italian musical term. Risk: **MEDIUM** (long; better as a feature or sub-brand).
- Meaning: the classic swell exercise (soft, then loud, then soft). The phrase translates as "placing the voice" — [Ars Electronica archive / search results](https://webarchive.ars.electronica.art/en/festival2003/updates/article.asp%3FiNewsID=312&iTypeID=9.html)
- Conflicts: no consumer singing app of that name was found. A 2003 Golan Levin and Zach Lieberman art project called *Messa di Voce* used voice-analysis software, which is prior art in voice visualisation but not a commercial brand — [Rhizome](https://rhizome.org/editorial/2009/feb/23/virtuosos-of-voice/)
- Domains: messadivoce.com was registered in 2002 (redirect page). .app and .ai are free.
- Fit: a natural sub-brand for the dynamics/expression module.

### Inferences
- The strongest overall master-brand pairing appears to be **Vocal Verdict** (clean, everything open) or **Singerprint** (more distinctive, but the .com must be acquired or a get-/try- prefix used).
- **Appoggio** is the most "insider-credible" evocative name still unclaimed in voice software. It would sit well with Italian-term sub-brands such as "Appoggio Polish" or "Appoggio Live", though everyday users would find it harder to spell.
- Italian terms suit sub-brands for individual measures (Squillo = resonance ring, Messa di Voce = dynamics, Passaggio = registers) even where they are unsafe as a master brand.
- Coined names (Singerprint, Takecard) are inherently stronger trademarks than descriptive compounds ("Vocal ..."), based on standard trademark-spectrum reasoning. I did not cite a source for this.

### Gaps
- No trademark-register hits (USPTO, IP Australia, EUIPO) were retrieved for any candidate. The search tool is US-only and register pages did not surface. A direct register search is required before choosing.
- Owners of the registered-but-dormant domains (singerprint.com, takecard.com, tenmark.com) are unknown, and so is their resale pricing.
- I checked Apple App Store presence only through web search, not by directly querying App Store or Google Play search pages.
- .com.au availability (important for an Australian business) was not checked.

## Which obvious candidates are already taken?

### Takeaway
Almost every obvious single-word musical or voice term is taken or crowded. Tessitura (major arts CRM), Formant (Formant Robotics), Cantabile (VST host software and a vocal group), Tenuto (musictheory.net app), Diapason (French music magazine and awards), Intona (audio hardware and an AI speech coach), Passaggio (piano-practice app; the near-identical "Passaggi" is a music app), Voce (VoceVista, an established singing-voice analysis program) and Squillo (AI company, plus a "call girl" slang meaning) are all compromised. Timbre, Aria, Resonance and Canto showed no exact-name singing app, but their .com domains are long gone and the words are generic.

### Cited Findings

**Squillo** (Italian for the "ring" or singer's formant). Risk: **MEDIUM–HIGH** as a master brand.
- squillo.com is used by "Squillo", a free agentic AI operating system ("This is how we AI"). That is software, so classes 9/42 may overlap, though the market differs — [squillo.com](https://squillo.com)
- No singing app named Squillo was found. A Bel Canto app (Debra Lynn) uses the word descriptively for the ring — [App Store: Debra Lynn Bel Canto](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1606863623)
- Connotation risk: masculine *lo squillo* means a phone ring, but feminine *la squillo* is shorthand for *ragazza squillo*, meaning "call girl" — [The Local Italy](https://www.thelocal.it/20181214/italian-word-of-the-day-squillo); [Daily Italian Words](https://dailyitalianwords.com/italian-word-squillo-ring/)
- Domains: squillo.com was registered in 2004 and is in use. squillo.app is registered; squillo.ai, squillovocal.com and getsquillo.com are free.
- Usable as an in-product metric label ("squillo / ring score"), not as a master brand.

**Passaggio** (register transition). Risk: **HIGH**.
- passaggio.app hosts "Passaggio — Track your piano journey", a practice log with recordings and a metronome. That is the adjacent music-practice space — [passaggio.app](https://passaggio.app)
- "Passaggi" is an iOS/Android improvisation app by Septenary Editions (US$14.99) — [App Store id1499123312](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1499123312); [Early Music America](https://www.earlymusicamerica.org/passaggi-for-ios-and-android-is-out-now/)
- passaggio.com has been registered since 1998 (no live site).

**Tessitura** (vocal range). Risk: **HIGH**.
- Tessitura is nonprofit arts ticketing/CRM software. It was developed by the Metropolitan Opera and has been maintained by Tessitura Network since 2001, with roughly 700–800+ client organisations. It is entrenched in opera and music — [Wikipedia: Tessitura (software)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tessitura_(software))
- tessitura.com has been registered since 2002.

**Formant.** Risk: **HIGH**.
- formant.app hosts "Formant Robotics". formant.com (2010) and formant.ai are both registered — RDAP plus [formant.app](https://formant.app)
- SonicPrint, a formant-based vocal training app (F1/F2 vowel resonance), already occupies the "formant" concept in singing apps — [App Store: SonicPrint](https://apps.apple.com/app/id491617264)

**Tenuto.** Risk: **HIGH**.
- Tenuto is an established musictheory.net iOS app (US$4.99, released Aug 2011, about 778 ratings at 4.74) — [musictheory.net: Tenuto](https://www.musictheory.net/buy/tenuto); [AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/459313476)

**Cantabile.** Risk: **HIGH**.
- Cantabile is a Windows VST host and live-performance music software from Topten Software, offered in Lite, Solo and Performer tiers (free / $69 / $199). It is in the same class (9) and the same audience of musicians — [KVR Audio](https://www.kvraudio.com/product/cantabile_by_topten_software); [Age of Audio](https://ageofaudio.com/topten-software-cantabile/)
- cantabile.com belongs to The London Quartet, a vocal group active since 1982 — [cantabile.com](https://cantabile.com)

**Diapason** (tuning fork, or concert pitch). Risk: **HIGH**.
- Diapason is a French monthly classical music magazine owned by Mondadori. Its Diapason d'Or awards are well known — [Wikipedia: Diapason (magazine)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diapason_(magazine))
- All three domains are registered.

**Intona / Intonare.** Risk: **HIGH**.
- Intona Technology (Germany) makes USB galvanic isolators popular in audiophile audio — [Positive Feedback review](https://positive-feedback.com/reviews/hardware-reviews/intona-7054-x-galvanic-isolator/)
- intona.app is an AI-powered English-speaking coach with recordings "marked up" and progress tracking. That is close to this product's feedback model — [intona.app](https://intona.app)

**Voce / VoceVista.** Risk: **HIGH** for any "Voce" brand.
- VoceVista Video is established singing-voice analysis and feedback software, a direct conceptual competitor — [vocevista.com](https://www.vocevista.com/en)
- voce.com has been registered since 2003. No standalone "Voce" singing app was found — [search results](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6460034299)

**Vocalis.** Risk: **MEDIUM–HIGH**.
- Vocalis Health was an Israeli-American vocal-biomarker AI company formed in 2019 (merger plus a US$9M round). One directory lists it as having ceased operating in April 2021, which is unconfirmed — [PR Newswire](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/diagnosing-respiratory-and-cardiac-diseases-through-voice-vocalis-health-closes-merger-and-a-9m-investment-round-to-create-a-global-leader-in-vocal-biomarkers-300973212.html); [Startupim](https://startupim.com/company/vocalis-health)
- Registered trademarks may survive the company. vocalis.com has been registered since 1993.

**Cantor.** Risk: **MEDIUM–HIGH**.
- "Cantor" is an iPad music-controller app (2012, reported by CDM) — [CDM](https://cdm.link/2012/06/touch-ios-music-in-a-new-way-hands-on-with-cantor-for-users-inside-details-for-developers/)
- cantor.com has been registered since 1993. The name also strongly evokes Cantor Fitzgerald (my general knowledge, not verified in this session).

**Timbre.** Risk: **MEDIUM** (generic and crowded, though no exact singing app was found).
- No dedicated "Timbre" singing app was found. "Timbre" appears as a feature in, for example, Chorilo's voice trainer — [Chorilo help](https://www.chorilo.com/en/help/sheet-music/voice-trainer-timbre)
- timbre.com has been registered since 1994.

**Aria / Resonance.** Risk: **MEDIUM** (crowded generic words).
- No singing app called exactly "Aria" or "Resonance" surfaced. Resonance is a common feature label in existing apps such as Sing Sharp ("real-time pitch and resonance feedback") and Voice Builder — [App Store: Sing Sharp](https://apps.apple.com/app/id772052329); [App Store: Voice Builder](https://apps.apple.com/app/id537753638)
- Generic dictionary words are hard to own and to rank for.

**Note by Note** (requested angle). Risk: **HIGH** for ownability, though it is good as a tagline.
- notebynote.com (registered 2000) is "Note By Note – Music & Movement Studio". notebynote.app was recently registered via Namecheap — [notebynote.com](https://notebynote.com)
- *Note by Note: The Making of Steinway L1037* (2007) is a PBS-aired documentary, and Steinway hosts a "Note by Note" page — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Note_by_Note:_The_Making_of_Steinway_L1037); [Steinway](https://www.steinway.com/misc/note-by-note)
- Pakachoag Music School publishes a Substack called "Note by Note", and the Voice Teacher app advertises "Learn note by note" — [Substack](https://pakachoagmusic.substack.com/); [App Store: Voice Teacher](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1387165550)

**Vocal Gauge** ("measure" angle). Risk: **HIGH**.
- Vocal Gauge is an existing app at vocalgauge.com (registered 2021) that puts songs into your vocal range — [vocalgauge.com](https://vocalgauge.com)

**Pitchproof** (provenance/proof angle). Risk: **MEDIUM–HIGH**.
- pitchproof.app is "PitchProof", a pitch/presentation practice product ("meet the question before it meets you"). pitchproof.com (2015) and pitchproof.ai are both registered — [pitchproof.app](https://pitchproof.app)

**Centwise** ("cents" measurement angle). Risk: **MEDIUM**.
- centwise.com (1999), .app and .ai are all registered with no live site found (RDAP). "Cents" also reads as money, which suggests personal finance. No conflict search beyond domains was done.

**Touchmark** (provenance/hallmark angle) and **Plumbline** (measurement-true angle). Risk: **MEDIUM**.
- touchmark.com is Touchmark (senior living), a different class — [touchmark.com](https://touchmark.com)
- plumbline.com is a NZ designer bathware brand — [plumbline.com](https://plumbline.com)
- All .com/.app/.ai domains are registered for both. They are generic and weakly tied to voice.

### Inferences
- Single Italian musical terms make a crowded namespace for music software. Wherever a musical term looked attractive (Cantabile, Tenuto, Passaggio, Diapason, Intona), someone in music or audio already uses it. Treat any further single-term idea as likely taken until checked.
- VoceVista and SonicPrint show that established voice-analysis tools already hold the "Voce" and "formant/print" naming territory. "Singerprint" sits near SonicPrint's "-print" pattern, but the words differ, so the confusion risk looks low (my judgement).

### Gaps
- Pitchfork, Cadence and Tuner were not searched in this session. From general knowledge, Pitchfork is a major music publication and Cadence is used by Cadence Design Systems among others; both should be treated as taken but are unverified here. "Tuner" is generic and unregistrable alone.
- No search was run for Canto as a company (it may be used by a digital-asset-management vendor, but that is unverified).
- Trademark registration status was not confirmed for any of these. The conflicts above are marketplace uses found on the web.

## Names on the "provenance / verdict / scorecard" and "measure vs pros" angles

### Takeaway
On the verdict/provenance angle, **Vocal Verdict** is the clear winner on availability, and **Singerprint** is the most distinctive. On the "vs the pros" angle, **Tenmark** carries the "10 = a typical pro" idea but sits close to Amazon's former TenMarks. **Takecard** is the cleanest "scorecard" name. As for Vox variants, VoxVerdict already belongs to an awards site and Voxprint/Voxmark .com are long-registered, so Vox compounds bring no advantage.

### Cited Findings
- VoxVerdict: voxverdict.com (registered 18 Oct 2023) hosts "VoxVerdict Awards". voxverdict.app and .ai are free — [voxverdict.com](https://voxverdict.com). Risk: **MEDIUM**. Use as a master brand would collide with that site, and the Vox stem is being screened separately.
- Voxprint / Voxmark: voxprint.com (1996) and voxmark.com (2002) are registered with no live sites, and voxmark.app is registered (RDAP). Risk: **MEDIUM–HIGH**. "Voxprint" reads as voice biometrics.
- Songproof (provenance angle): songproof.com (registered Apr 2025) is a music copyright-registration service ("Your song's birth certificate"). The name is taken in the adjacent music space — [songproof.com](https://songproof.com)
- Takeproof: takeproof.com (2022) is an AI-photo app — [takeproof.com](https://takeproof.com)
- Typical competitor naming in singing apps is literal ("Vocal Pitch Monitor", "Voice Training Pro", "Vocal Coach Singer", "OnPitch", "Nail the Pitch"), so a verdict or evocative name would stand out — [App Store: OnPitch](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1212118228); [App Store: Nail the Pitch](https://apps.apple.com/ae/app/id1486640549)

### Inferences
Suggested shortlist, in my ranking with reasons:
1. **Vocal Verdict**: LOW risk; all domains free; the positioning is in the name. Sub-brands could be Vocal Verdict Polish and Vocal Verdict Live. Watch the tone: pair it with an encouraging tagline such as "a coach's report for every song you sing".
2. **Singerprint**: LOW–MEDIUM; the most ownable coined word, and .ai is free. The .com needs acquisition or a prefix such as getsingerprint.com.
3. **Appoggio**: LOW–MEDIUM; craft credibility, and the .com is purchasable at a known price (US$3,795). Harder for lay users.
4. **Takecard**: LOW–MEDIUM; clean and literal; .app/.ai free.
5. **Vocal Assay**: LOW conflict, but the word is less friendly.
6. **Tenmark**: MEDIUM; check Amazon's TenMarks registrations.
- Keep for feature or sub-brand names only: Squillo (resonance ring metric), Messa di Voce (dynamics), Passaggio (registers), and Note by Note (as a report-section title or tagline).
- Avoid: Tessitura, Formant, Cantabile, Tenuto, Diapason, Intona, Voce*, Vocalis, Vocal Gauge, Pitchproof, Songproof, Note by Note (as a brand).

### Gaps
- No sentiment or user testing on how "Verdict" reads to amateur singers. This is a tone risk identified by inference only.
- No check of social handles (Instagram, TikTok, YouTube) or Australian business-name registers (ASIC) for any candidate.
- Domain aftermarket prices were found only for appoggio.com. Prices for singerprint.com, takecard.com and tenmark.com are unknown.
