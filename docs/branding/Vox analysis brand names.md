# Retire VOX and build a name you own

**Recommendation: stop building on "VOX". Move the whole suite to one distinctive master brand, and use plain words under it: [Master] Analyze, [Master] Polish, [Master] Monitor.** The research supports three front-runners:

- **Vocent** (voice + *cent*, the 1/100-semitone unit the engine measures in). It is the best all-round master brand, if a clearance search comes back clean.
- **Tessimetry** (tessitura + -metry). It is the safest and cheapest: no conflicts were found, and the .com, .app and .ai were all unregistered on 14 Oct 2026.
- **Singerprint** (singer + fingerprint). It is the warmest name for consumers, but its .com has to be bought or worked around.

Why leave VOX? The word is already taken in exactly the places you would sell:

- **VOX Amplification** (Korg's guitar-amp brand) holds a US Class 9 "VOX" filing for musicians' audio gear.
- Plain "VOX" registrations in Classes 9 and 41 sit in the same US records as Vox Media's large portfolio.
- At least nine Vox-branded voice or singing tools already exist, including a singing-evaluation SDK.

In trademark terms, "VOX Analysis", "VoxPolish" and "Vox Suite" all boil down to "VOX plus a generic word". That makes them weak to defend and a likely problem in a buyer's due diligence if you ever sell. "Vox Suite" is also the weakest of the three: voxsuite.com has been registered since 2000.

The case for a new name is strongest now, while the product has few outside users and switching costs almost nothing.

**One honest limit runs through everything below.** It all rests on web searches and domain-registry lookups. No search of the official USPTO, EUIPO or IP Australia databases was possible, and the Australian and EU registers are effectively unchecked. Treat every "free" or "low risk" here as a lead to verify, not legal clearance.

## VOX is crowded, legally weak and already owned by bigger players

**The most serious conflict is the one closest to your users.** VOX Amplification Limited holds US application 90817520 (filed 2021, Class 9) for amplifiers and other audio equipment for musicians ([Justia](https://trademarks.justia.com/908/17/vox-90817520.html)). VOX has been owned by Korg since 1992 ([Wikipedia](https://www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/Vox_AC15)).

Vox Media is the second large player. It holds about **263 current US marks, including 136 in Class 41 and 22 in Class 9** ([GleanMark](https://gleanmark.com/owner/voxmediallc)). Separately, plain "VOX" registrations cover downloadable podcasts in Class 9 and podcast entertainment and educational events in Class 41 ([Justia 88299547](https://trademark.justia.com/882/99/vox-88299547.html); [Justia 87099702](https://trademarks.justia.com/870/99/vox-87099702.html)). The likely owner of those registrations is Vox Media, but that is unconfirmed.

A US filing for "VOX ANALYSIS" would land next to these marks. Class 9 covers downloadable software, Class 42 covers online software services, and Class 41 covers coaching. An examiner could refuse it for likelihood of confusion, or VOX/Korg could oppose it. That is an inference from the records, not an examiner's finding.

The marketplace is just as crowded as the register. Several products already do almost exactly what yours does:

- **VoxaTrace**: an SDK for singing evaluation and vocal-quality analysis ([klibs.io](https://klibs.io/project/musicmuni/voxatrace)).
- **VoxApp – Voice Analysis** ([mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/pt/apps/voxapp-voice-analysis/6760035609)).
- **VoxMetria**: a clinical voice-analysis tool ([appshunter](https://appshunter.io/ios/app/voxmetria/id824941408)).
- **Voxit**: UC Davis's open-source toolkit for speech and song analysis ([GitHub](https://github.com/MillerLab-UCDavis/Voxit)).
- **Vox Tools: Learn to Sing**, with about 558 ratings ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/vox-tools-learn-to-sing/id568721281)).

VoxPolish has its own neighbour: Voxengo's **Voxformer** vocal channel strip ([Syntex](https://syntex.tv/voxengo-voxformer)). Closer to home, **NCH Software, an Australian company, sells the Voxal voice changer** ([VoxBooster review](https://voxbooster.com/de/blog/voxal-review/)). That makes an IP Australia check essential before any Australian filing.

Search crowding makes discovery worse still. vox.com and the amps dominate the bare word, and AI speech models such as Mistral's Voxtral and OpenBMB's VoxCPM fill the voice news cycle ([Mistral](https://mistral.ai/news/voxtral-tts/); [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/openbmb/VoxCPM2)).

**The Latin root does not rescue it either.** US law sometimes treats words from dead languages as too unfamiliar for buyers to translate ([Wikipedia: doctrine of foreign equivalents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctrine_of_foreign_equivalents)). But musicians use "vox" to mean "vocals" every day, so for vocal software the word is at best highly suggestive. That makes it conceptually weak: you could not stop others from using Vox-something, while the senior VOX owners could still object to you.

| Current name | Exact-name product found? | .com (RDAP, 14 Oct 2026) | Main problem | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| VOX Analysis | No | Unregistered | Generic suffix; sits among VoxaTrace, VoxApp, VoxMetria, Voxit | Moderate legal risk, low ownability |
| VoxPolish | No | Unregistered | Overlaps with Voxengo/Voxformer vocal processing | Strongest of the three, still VOX-led |
| Vox Suite | No | **Registered since 2000** ([RDAP](https://rdap.verisign.com/com/v1/domain/voxsuite.com)) | "Suite" adds nothing; .com unavailable | Weakest |

The free .com domains are a false comfort. In a confusion analysis, the dominant word, VOX, is what gets compared with earlier marks.

## Winning names in this market are distinctive, not descriptive

**Consumer singing apps mostly use near-interchangeable descriptive names, and that is exactly why a distinctive name stands out.** The category is full of Sing-, Vocal-, Voice- and Pitch- compounds: SingSharp, Simply Sing, Singing Coach AI, Rate My Singing, VocalTuner, and at least three apps called some version of "Vocal Pitch Monitor" ([Singing Carrots blog](https://singingcarrots.com/blog/top-7-ai-vocal-coaches/); [App Store: VocalTuner](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/vocaltuner-pitch-training/id1505735245); [App Store: Voice Whiz](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/vocal-pitch-monitor-voice-whiz/id1579828873)).

The names that endured are coined or arbitrary: Smule, Yousician, Melodyne and Auto-Tune. **Smule even dropped its descriptive launch name, "Sing! Karaoke", for the coined company name** ([Wikipedia: Smule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smule_(app))). People now use Melodyne as a verb, alongside Auto-Tune ([Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/auto-tune-vs-melodyne)).

The flip side is enforcement. **In 2009 Antares forced an iPhone app called "AutoTuner" to rename**, and the small developer could not afford to fight ([Digital Music News](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2009/11/10/autotune/)). That is the position a VOX-named product would be in, from the weaker side.

Trademark law ranks names on a spectrum: **fanciful (invented) > arbitrary > suggestive > descriptive > generic**. Descriptive names need proof of acquired distinctiveness before they can be registered ([DRM](https://www.drm.com/articles/trademarks-the-spectrum-of-distinctiveness/)). A quick test: a name that answers "what is it?" leans descriptive, and one that answers "who is it?" is a brand ([Trembly Law](https://tremblylaw.com/blog/what-are-some-characteristics-of-a-strong-trademark/)).

Practical SaaS naming guidance adds a few rules ([Spaceship](https://www.spaceship.com/blog/saas-product-name-guide/); [DomainDetails](https://domaindetails.com/kb/getting-started/saas-domain-naming-strategy)):

- Keep it short and spelled the way it sounds.
- Avoid hyphens and numbers.
- Make sure the name won't lock you into one product.
- Domains: about **95% of 550+ SaaS companies use .com**, but **28% of YC Winter 2024 startups chose .ai**. Starting on .app or .ai is acceptable if you plan to buy the .com later.

**There is also an open positioning lane.** Every competitor calls itself a "coach" or "trainer". Rate My Singing (0–100 scores) and Bloom Vocal (a five-category rubric) come closest to giving a verdict ([Bloom Vocal](https://www.bloomvocal.site/en/blog/best-ai-vocal-coach-apps-2026)). But no competitor found brands itself on measurement credibility or a professional benchmark. The scientific tools name themselves like instruments (VoceVista, Overtone Analyzer) ([VoceVista](https://www.vocevista.com/en/about/)).

That points to a name with an instrument's precision and a singer's warmth. The three front-runners all aim there.

### One master brand with plain descriptors fits a solo developer

Audio companies split between two models:

- **House of brands.** iZotope sells Ozone, Nectar and RX as separate names. When Boris FX acquired iZotope in July 2026, licences stayed active and no products were renamed ([MusicTech](https://musictech.com/news/industry/izotope-joins-boris-fx-exits-native-instruments/)). Muse Group kept Audacity, MuseScore and Ultimate Guitar under their own names ([MusicTech](https://musictech.com/news/audacity-acquired-muse-group-ultimate-guitar-musescore/)).
- **Branded house.** JoyTunes renamed itself "Simply" in July 2022 to match Simply Piano, Simply Guitar and Simply Sing ([Wikipedia: Simply](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simply_(software_company))).

Weak umbrella names get thrown away. "Soundwide" was dropped for "Native Instruments" in 2023 because more people knew NI ([RouteNote](https://routenote.com/blog/izotope-brainworx-plugin-alliance-join-native-instruments/)).

**For one person running three tools, the branded house wins:** one distinctive master name, with generic descriptors underneath, so all the brand value builds up in a single mark you can sell. The descriptors (Analyze, Polish, Monitor) are generic. They do no trademark work and need no clearance.

## Vocent, Tessimetry and Singerprint lead the shortlist

**1. Vocent: the best master brand, if it clears.** It is short, two syllables (VOH-sent), and works on two levels. A casual user hears "voice", and a musician or engineer catches the *cents* pun, which signals the precision behind the positioning. It also keeps a soft echo of "Vox", which makes it easier for anyone who already knows the old name.

It carries the suite cleanly: **Vocent Analyze, Vocent Polish, Vocent Monitor**. The costs and risks:

- **The .com costs US$3,988.**
- **The .ai is taken.**
- **Vocent Solutions' old trademark history has to be checked** for dead or assigned marks before you buy anything.
- It sits in the crowded "Voc-" prefix zone (Vocaberry, Vocular, VocAlign, Voloco), so it needs a careful Class 9 confusion check.
- For the EU search, also check **Vocento**, a Spanish media group name. It comes from general knowledge and was not verified in this research.

Taglines:

- *"Your voice, measured to the cent."*
- *"Measured to the cent. Judged against the pros."*

**2. Tessimetry: the safest choice today.** It is invented, no conflicts were found, and **the .com, .app and .ai were all unregistered**, so the full set costs only standard registration fees. Trained singers know tessitura, and "-metry" says measurement outright. That fits "scientific-grade measurement with a verdict on top" better than any other name screened.

Its weakness is reach. At four syllables it is heavier for karaoke users. It is also a natural name for an analysis product but a stiff umbrella: "Tessimetry Polish" reads less naturally than "Vocent Polish".

Taglines:

- *"The science of your voice, with a verdict on top."*
- *"Scored against 50 professional vocals. 10 = a typical pro."*

**3. Singerprint: the warmest consumer name.** It is memorable, friendly and distinctive. The "print" idea fits a deterministic engine: the same audio gets the same score, and every take leaves its mark.

The catch is domains. **The .com and .app are held by a third party** with no live site, so you would need to buy them or launch on singerprint.ai or a prefix such as getsingerprint.com. Do a quick check against SonicPrint, a formant-training app ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id491617264)), although the words differ enough that confusion looks unlikely.

Taglines:

- *"Every take leaves a print."*
- *"Know your voice by its print."*

**How to decide:** run the clearance check on Vocent first. If it comes back clean and the US$3,988 is acceptable, choose Vocent. If clearance raises a problem, or you would rather spend nothing, choose Tessimetry. If you see the market mainly as amateur and karaoke singers rather than serious vocalists, choose Singerprint instead.

**What happens to the existing VOX names:**

- **Drop VOX from every public name.** The master brand alone names the suite, so there is no "Suite" product.
- **The apps become [Master] Analyze, [Master] Polish and [Master] Monitor.** "Fused" stays a mode inside the app, not a brand.
- **The repo can stay as it is.** The `voxanalysis/` and `voxsuite/` directories are internal and never seen by customers, so there is no need to churn the code.
- **For existing users,** say "Vocent Analyze (formerly VOX Analysis)" for a release or two.

Two good ideas from other candidates live on as feature names, where trademark weight does not matter:

- **"The Verdict"** as the name of the report's headline section, e.g. "the Vocent Verdict". This keeps Vocal Verdict's strong positioning without its weak trademark.
- **Italian craft terms** as metric labels: Appoggio for breath support, Messa di Voce for dynamics, Squillo for resonance ring. These are safe as descriptive labels inside the product, even where they are unsafe as brands.

## Six steps turn a shortlist into a name you own

Do these in order. The first is cheap insurance; the fourth is where real legal certainty comes from.

| # | Step | Where | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Register the free domains for your front-runners this week**: tessimetry.com/.app/.ai, vocent.app, singerprint.ai. Hold off on buying vocent.com until step 2 is done | Any registrar | Unregistered names in this naming territory are being taken (two in Apr 2026) |
| 2 | **Run your own knockout searches** for the exact name and sound-alikes (Vocent/Voxent/Vocento; Tessi*/Tessitura) in **Classes 9, 41 and 42** | IP Australia trade mark search; USPTO Trademark Search plus TSDR for status and owner; EUIPO eSearch / TMview; WIPO Global Brand Database | The research could not reach these databases. This is the gap that matters most, especially for Australia and the EU |
| 3 | **Search the stores and social handles directly** | App Store, Google Play, Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, X | Store presence was checked only indirectly through web search |
| 4 | **Check Australian business details** | ASIC business-names register; .com.au availability (needs an ABN) | Neither was checked in this research |
| 5 | **Get a clearance opinion from a registered Australian trade marks attorney** before filing or paying US$3,988. Ask about filing in Australia first and extending to the US and EU through the Madrid system | IP Australia lists registered attorneys | A web screen cannot judge likelihood of confusion; an attorney can, and a buyer's due diligence will expect it |
| 6 | **Say the name aloud to five singers** and ask them to spell it back | Your own network | The "radio test" catches Vo-scent / sing-a-list problems before launch; no user testing was done in this research |

Stop launching anything new under VOX names while this runs. Every new public use deepens the switching cost and adds to the VOX conflict.

## Conclusion

The naming question turns out to be about what the product can be worth, not about taste. As "VOX Analysis", it would launch as one more entry in a crowded field, next to companies with deeper pockets and earlier rights. As a distinctive mark you own, the name becomes something a buyer actually pays for. The 2026 iZotope sale is the reminder: strong product names travel intact through acquisitions, and weak umbrella names get thrown away.

The second insight is about positioning. The "10 = a typical pro" benchmark is an open lane that no competitor's name or brand claims. Vocent and Tessimetry both put measurement in the name. Whichever wins should be paired with taglines and a "Verdict" feature that spell out the benchmark, and that should happen before someone else registers the obvious words.
