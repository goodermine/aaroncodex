## Core claims (summary)

- **Two different things get called "falsetto."** *Weak/breathy falsetto* —
  folds vibrate along the inner edges with reduced contact, more open than
  closed per cycle, so more air escapes: breathy, low-intensity, few upper
  harmonics, cricothyroid-dominated. *Reinforced falsetto* — the **same
  fundamental vibratory pattern** but with increased fold closure, breath
  compression and resonance, giving a stronger, clearer, harmonically richer
  sound with a "blade" or "laser" quality.
- **Reinforced falsetto is ~90% of the full voice** — a template. The missing
  10% is slightly more firmness in the folds, added *without* leaving the
  reinforced-falsetto posture, which then connects to full voice while keeping
  the same ease.
- **It is not chest voice carried higher.** If it were, it would stop being
  falsetto. The vibratory pattern stays falsetto-like; it just becomes stronger,
  more connected, more efficient.
- **The mechanism is efficiency, not force.** Not more air — *better-managed*
  air. Longer closed phase → mild subglottic pressure → the "stronger" feeling.
  Trying to produce it by blowing harder blows the folds open; the instruction
  is "do less."
- **The developmental sequence:** cultivate chest voice correctly → ascend into
  reinforced falsetto → mixed coordination → full voice, bottom to top.
  Reinforced falsetto is the "second floor / bridge / staircase" to the high
  register. Pushing and grunting skips it and shortens/thickens the folds back
  into a strained state.
- **The break is the key, not the enemy.** The yodel/crack between chest and
  reinforced falsetto is where the full voice is found; it should be gentle, not
  avoided.

## Practice content the video gives

- **Slow, patient morning warm-ups** built from reinforced falsetto, never
  pushed — full voice "appears by itself."
- **Reinforced falsetto → yawn-like sigh ("woo")** on a long deep vowel to
  elongate the vocal tract, then bring the thin-edge connection down toward
  chest.
- **Finding it via play:** the "woo"/holler of a rollercoaster or celebration is
  already reinforced falsetto; fine-tune that to a pitch. Add an **NG** position;
  find the "cry / buzz / focus" of a boyish cry.
- **Messa di voce** — crescendo/decrescendo from weak → reinforced falsetto
  without pushing.
- **SOVT / straw-in-water** — trains the focused reinforced-falsetto sound and
  the sensation of subglottic pressure from folds staying in contact; the "slight
  change to a whistle" versus just blowing air.
- **Train with it, don't perform with it** — the gym analogy: specific,
  isolated coordination work, not a stage sound.
- **Historic appeals to authority:** Corelli, Melocchi, Pavarotti ("let them
  spin"), Loforese — the upper register as release and coordination, not brute
  force. (Attributed by the speaker; not independently verified here.)
