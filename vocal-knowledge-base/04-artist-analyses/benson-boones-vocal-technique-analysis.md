# Benson Boone's Vocal Technique Analysis

## Benson Boone: A Vocal Phenomenon and the Anatomy of "Beautiful Things"

### Introduction

Benson Boone has rapidly emerged as a significant new voice in the contemporary pop music landscape. From his initial visibility on social media platforms to achieving global chart success, his trajectory warrants detailed examination. This report will analyze Benson Boone's artistic development, with a particular focus on his distinct vocal technique. Furthermore, it will provide an in-depth deconstruction of his hit single, "Beautiful Things," examining its lyrical content, musical structure, commercial performance, and critical reception. The analysis will also extend to his live performances and the broader artistic identity he is cultivating, offering a comprehensive understanding of his current position and potential future impact within popular music.

### 1. The Ascent of Benson Boone: From TikTok to Global Stages

Benson Boone's journey into the music industry is emblematic of a contemporary pathway to stardom, heavily influenced by social media and direct audience engagement.

- **Early Life and Discovery:** Hailing from Monroe, Washington, Boone's initial musical explorations were relatively informal. A pivotal moment in his early musical development was attending a Jon Bellion concert, which inspired him to take singing more seriously. On a friend's recommendation, he began posting singing videos on TikTok, a platform where his "soulful voice and magnetic presence" quickly captivated millions. This burgeoning online popularity led him to audition for season 19 of *American Idol* in early 2021.

- **The *American Idol* Decision and Independent Path:** Boone made a notable impact on *American Idol*, with judges recognizing his raw talent. However, in a move that surprised many, he chose to withdraw from the competition. This decision, described as a bold move to "seize control of his destiny and forge his own path" , underscores a desire for artistic autonomy often seen in new generations of artists who have alternative routes to industry recognition. This departure allowed him to sign with Night Street Records, a label under Warner Records founded by Dan Reynolds of Imagine Dragons, providing him with major label support while ostensibly allowing for more creative control than a post-*Idol* contract might have offered.

- **Early Career Development and Breakthrough:** Following his *American Idol* stint, Boone focused on releasing original music. His debut single, "Ghost Town," released in late 2021, achieved significant chart success, signaling his potential beyond viral moments. He continued to build his discography with subsequent singles and EPs, consistently leveraging his strong social media presence to connect with a growing fanbase. His music often explores themes of love, heartbreak, and life's emotional complexities, resonating particularly with a younger demographic.The rapid ascent from a small-town talent posting on TikTok to an artist with chart-topping singles illustrates a significant shift in how musical talent can be discovered and cultivated. The traditional gatekeepers of the music industry, while still influential, are increasingly sharing space with algorithm-driven platforms that can catapult artists to fame based on direct audience appeal. Boone's narrative is a clear example of this phenomenon, where viral success on platforms like TikTok served as a powerful launchpad, bypassing some of the conventional stepping stones to a music career. His ability to translate this online engagement into tangible commercial success with "Ghost Town" and later, more spectacularly, with "Beautiful Things," indicates not just fleeting internet fame but the cultivation of a dedicated fanbase and an artistic identity that resonates.

### 2. Vocal Profile and Technique: The Signature Sound of Benson Boone

Benson Boone's vocal delivery is a central component of his artistic identity, characterized by a blend of raw power, emotional intensity, and specific technical choices that create a distinctive, if sometimes polarizing, sound.

- **Vocal Range and Type:** Boone possesses a notable vocal range, documented as spanning from G3 to F6, covering approximately 2.8 octaves. This allows him considerable flexibility in navigating melodies across different registers. While a formal classification of his voice type (e.g., tenor, baritone) is less discussed in the available materials, his ability to hit high notes with power, particularly in the F6 region as demonstrated in "Beautiful Things" , suggests a high tenor or a baritone with exceptional upper extension.

- **Core Vocal Techniques:**

  - **Rasp and Vocal Fry:** A prominent feature of Boone's singing is his use of rasp, often achieved through controlled vocal fry. This technique adds an edgy, emotional texture to his voice, particularly evident in climactic sections of his songs. Vocal coach Adam Mishan notes that in "Beautiful Things," Boone adds rasp for "emotional weight" in the chorus. While effective for conveying intensity, Mishan also cautions that rasp requires control to avoid strain and should be used strategically.

  - **Dynamics (Crescendos and Diminuendos):** Boone demonstrates a sophisticated use of dynamics. Mishan highlights his employment of diminuendos (softening the voice) to pull back energy, followed by crescendos (gradually building intensity) to create a "push-and-pull effect" that draws listeners in. This dynamic interplay is crucial to the emotional arc of his songs.

  - **Register Transitions:** Boone navigates different vocal registers, including falsetto, a "gentle mix," and a powerful belt. His ability to transition between these, for instance, moving from softer verses into a full-voiced, high-octane chorus, is a key element of his style. The leap of an octave into the chorus of "Beautiful Things" is a prime example.

  - **Vibrato:** Some listeners perceive a pronounced vibrato in Boone's singing. One review describes it as "ramping his voice up to eleven with vibrato that sounds like it's too cold in the recording studio" in softer sections, before transitioning to more powerful, screamed vocals. The quality and application of his vibrato can contribute to the perceived intensity of his delivery.

  - **Belting:** Boone frequently employs a powerful belting technique, especially in the choruses of his songs. This is described as taking a speech-like quality higher with excitement or anger, leaning towards a "gritty, twangier quality". This approach contributes to the "screaming" quality some listeners identify.

- **Vocal Training and Development:** Boone's early musical experiences included participation in school choirs, which likely provided a foundational understanding of vocalization. Initially, his development was largely self-directed. However, as his commitment to music grew, he sought formal singing lessons to refine his technique and expand his vocal range. The guidance of a singing coach is credited with playing a "significant role in honing his natural talent". This combination of natural aptitude, early informal training, and later professional coaching has shaped his current vocal capabilities.The combination of Boone's wide vocal range and his willingness to push his voice to its emotional and dynamic limits creates a signature sound that is immediately recognizable. This vocal signature is not merely about hitting high notes or employing rasp; it's about the *way* these elements are combined to serve the emotional narrative of his songs. The rawness in his delivery, which can be perceived as both a strength (conveying authentic emotion) and a weakness (sounding "shrill" or "irritating" to some ), is a direct result of these technical choices. His formal training likely provided him with the control to deploy these intense vocalizations, but his artistic choice leans towards a less polished, more visceral expression. This careful balance between trained capability and raw delivery is fundamental to his appeal and the discussions surrounding his vocal merit.

### 3. Deconstructing "Beautiful Things": Anatomy of a Global Hit

Benson Boone's single "Beautiful Things" stands as a pivotal track in his career, catapulting him to new heights of global recognition. An analysis of the song reveals a carefully constructed piece that combines relatable lyrical themes with a dynamic vocal and musical arrangement.

- **Song Information: Genesis and Release:** "Beautiful Things" was released on January 19, 2024, through Night Street Records and Warner Records, serving as the lead single from his debut studio album, *Fireworks & Rollerblades* (2024). The song was co-written by Boone with Jack LaFrantz and Evan Blair, with Blair also handling production. Boone began writing the song on September 29, 2023, after moving to Los Angeles, combining elements from two different song ideas at LaFrantz's suggestion. He reportedly wrote the melodies on his grandmother's old piano. The song is described as a "ballad about the meaning of life" with "soulful verses" and a "powerful chorus," set in the key of B-flat major.

- **Lyrical Themes and Meaning: Vulnerability and Fear of Loss:** The lyrical core of "Beautiful Things" revolves around gratitude for newfound love and happiness, juxtaposed with an intense fear of losing these precious aspects of life. Boone described the song as being inspired by a new relationship where, for the first time, he felt "genuinely terrified to lose something". The lyrics are a direct plea: "Please stay / I want you, I need you, oh God / Don't take / These beautiful things that I've got". This vulnerability is a conscious artistic choice, as Boone states he is "very comfortable in being vulnerable" in his music. The song also features direct addresses to God, expressing gratitude but also acknowledging divine power to give and take away. The shift in tempo and intensity from the verses to the chorus is meant to reflect the mindset of appreciating a good thing while simultaneously fearing its potential loss.

- **Commercial Performance and Chart Dominance:** "Beautiful Things" achieved remarkable commercial success. It peaked at number two on the Billboard Hot 100 in the United States and topped the charts in at least 19 other countries, including Australia, Austria, Canada, France, Germany, Ireland, New Zealand, and the United Kingdom. Billboard even declared it "the biggest song in the world" at one point in 2024. A significant factor in its success was a strategic pre-release campaign on TikTok and Instagram, where Boone steadily teased the song, amassing over 130 million views for its sound before its official release. This built considerable anticipation, leading to the music video garnering half a million views within an hour of its premiere. This pre-release strategy demonstrates a clear causal relationship between targeted social media engagement and subsequent explosive chart performance, creating massive demand that translated directly into high streams and sales.

- **Reception: Critical Acclaim and Audience Reactions:** The reception to "Beautiful Things" has been multifaceted. Positive reviews praise its relatable message of fearing loss, Boone's "satisfying vocals," the "explosive chorus," and the song's dynamic range. Vocal coach Adam Mishan lauded it as a "masterclass in dynamics, melody, and storytelling". However, the song has also faced criticism. Some listeners find the lyrics "annoying" or lacking deeper reflection , while others question the theological implications of Boone's pleas to God. The most significant point of contention is Boone's vocal delivery in the chorus, which has been described as "irritating," "shrill," "caterwauling," or like he's "throwing a tantrum". The song's sound has also drawn comparisons to "a big old Christian song" , a perception amplified by Boone's Mormon background and direct faith references. This characterization is viewed positively by some and negatively by others.The immense success of "Beautiful Things" can be partly attributed to the powerful congruence between its lyrical themes and vocal execution. The raw desperation and fear of loss articulated in the lyrics are mirrored and amplified by the vocal delivery, which builds from vulnerable, softer verses to the impassioned, almost "screaming" plea of the chorus. This synergy creates an intense emotional package that clearly resonates with a large audience, even if the intensity itself is divisive. Furthermore, the song's overt lyrical engagement with faith, particularly the direct addresses to God concerning the blessings and potential losses in life , is quite pronounced for a mainstream pop hit. This may indicate a growing comfort among artists in expressing specific faith perspectives or an increased audience receptivity to such themes when embedded within relatable emotional struggles, potentially signaling a broader trend in popular music.

### 4. The Live Experience: Energy, Acrobatics, and Connection

Benson Boone's live performances are becoming as much a part of his artistic signature as his recorded music, characterized by high energy, surprising physicality, and a concerted effort to connect with his audience.

- **Translating Studio Sound to Stage:** Reviews of Boone's live shows suggest a successful translation of his studio sound to the stage. His vocals are often described as "flawlessly delivered," with the microphone "on and loud," ensuring clarity and impact. He demonstrates an ability to maintain vocal quality despite engaging in significant physical exertion, a testament to his stamina and control. It has been noted by some observers that he occasionally performs songs in a key a half-step lower than the studio versions. This is a common practice among vocalists during live tours to preserve vocal health or to facilitate ease of delivery over extended periods.

- **Showmanship and Stage Presence:** Boone's stage presence is frequently highlighted as a major draw. His energy is described as "intoxicating" and has even drawn comparisons to "the late Freddie Mercury" in its commanding nature. He is known for continuous movement, running on stage, and, most notably, his acrobatics. Backflips and front flips, often executed off pianos or other stage elements, are a unique and memorable feature of his performances. Beyond the spectacle, Boone actively engages with his audience. He makes efforts to connect on a personal level, such as asking for fans' names and reaching out to them from the stage. He encourages singalongs, fostering a communal atmosphere , and often closes his shows by interacting directly with front-row fans. His live productions are also noted for their scale, featuring LED screens, sophisticated lighting rigs, and a full live band, contributing to an "arena-ready" feel.

- **Vocal Stamina and Consistency in Live Settings:** The physical demands of Boone's performances necessitate considerable vocal stamina. He has been described as a "vocal athlete," with his fitness being crucial for executing his energetic shows. His capacity to deliver powerful and consistent vocals while being highly active, including performing acrobatics, is frequently praised. Vocal analysts have commended his ability to make demanding performances his own, preserving the charisma and energy of the song while adapting it for the live environment.A fascinating aspect of Boone's live persona is the juxtaposition of his physical exuberance with the often introspective and emotionally heavy nature of his songs, such as "In The Stars" or "Beautiful Things". One review explicitly calls him an "anomaly" for singing "deep and powerful songs, yet backflips off pianos like nothing". This unexpected combination subverts typical expectations for a singer-songwriter known for emotional ballads and could be a key component of his unique appeal, particularly to a younger audience accustomed to high-stimulus entertainment. This approach may also be indicative of a broader shift in the "pop balladeer" persona. Traditionally, artists in this vein might adopt a more static stage presence, focusing primarily on emotive vocal delivery. Boone's pronounced physicality incorporates elements more commonly associated with rock showmanship or even athletic performance, potentially influencing audience expectations for live shows within this genre. His evident physical fitness, which allows for continuous running and acrobatics , is almost certainly a direct contributor to his ability to maintain vocal consistency and power throughout such demanding performances. The core strength and breath support required for such activity are foundational to sustaining vocal quality under physical duress.

### 5. Musical Influences and Artistic Identity

Benson Boone's artistic identity is shaped by a combination of his acknowledged musical influences, comparisons drawn by critics and fans, and a recurring perception of his music aligning with a particular spiritual or stylistic aesthetic.

- **Acknowledged Influences:** Boone has cited Jon Bellion as a primary early musical inspiration, the experience of attending Bellion's concert being a catalyst for him to pursue singing more seriously. He also points to a range of older artists who formed his musical "core" during his upbringing, including Elton John, Billy Joel, Stevie Wonder, and Aretha Franklin. He has expressed an aspiration to create a "modern version of that" sound, aiming for a "pop soul vibe" in his own music.

- **Comparisons to Other Artists by Critics/Fans:** Listeners and critics have drawn various comparisons to contextualize Boone's sound. Some are generally positive or neutral, likening him to artists such as Harry Styles, Noah Kahan, Thomas Day, and Matt Hansen. However, other comparisons are more critical or highlight divisive aspects of his style. He has been unfavorably compared to Lewis Capaldi by some , and also likened to Shawn Mendes, Imagine Dragons, and even Nickelback by detractors. These varied comparisons underscore the difficulty in neatly categorizing his music and the range of reactions his style elicits.

- **The "Christian Pop" or "Megachurch" Aesthetic:** A significant and recurring theme in discussions of Boone's music is its perceived similarity in sound, lyrical approach, or emotional intensity to Contemporary Christian Music (CCM) or the "pomp of a megachurch". His Mormon background is often referenced in this context , and his lyrics frequently include direct references to God and themes of faith. This aspect of his artistry is polarizing: some may appreciate the perceived sincerity or find the spiritual undertones uplifting, while others may find it "preachy," out of place in secular pop, or simply not to their taste.An interesting tension arises between Boone's stated aspiration for a "pop soul vibe" inspired by classic soul artists and the frequent perception of his music aligning more closely with CCM or "megachurch" pop. While soul music historically has deep roots in gospel and spiritual expression, the contemporary iteration Boone presents—characterized by its specific lyrical content addressing faith and its intense, sometimes overwhelming emotional delivery —leads many listeners to the CCM comparison. This disconnect may stem from how "soul" is interpreted; if the focus is on raw, emotional outpouring, it can sonically and affectively overlap with styles prevalent in contemporary Christian worship. Alternatively, listeners might be reacting more to the explicit lyrical content than to purely sonic elements when drawing this parallel. This divergence between his artistic goals and public/critical reception is a key element shaping his current artistic identity. Boone's mainstream success with overtly faith-inflected lyrics, as seen in "Beautiful Things" , could also signify a broader cultural trend where the traditional distinctions between secular pop and CCM are becoming less rigid. It might suggest that artists feel more comfortable integrating personal faith into mainstream-targeted music, or that mainstream audiences are increasingly receptive to such themes, especially when they are framed within universally relatable emotional struggles. Furthermore, there's a potential contradiction between his cited influences of "older music" from artists like Elton John and Stevie Wonder , known for their sophisticated musicianship and nuanced vocal performances, and the occasional critical label of his vocal style as having a "Zoomer voice". This term, often used to describe certain contemporary vocal affectations and trends popular among younger artists, suggests that while his foundational inspirations might be classic, his execution is filtered through very current pop vocal trends, production choices, and stylistic mannerisms. His delivery may meld the emotionality drawn from older influences with modern pop sensibilities.

### 6. Concluding Analysis: Benson Boone's Place in the Pop Landscape

Benson Boone has carved out a distinctive niche in the current pop music scene, characterized by a potent combination of emotional vulnerability, powerful vocal dynamics, and a unique performance style. His rapid rise, fueled by social media savvy and genuine audience connection, particularly with a younger demographic, underscores his immediate impact. The global success of "Beautiful Things" attests to his ability to craft memorable melodies and emotionally resonant lyrical themes. His energetic and acrobatic live performances further differentiate him, offering a high-octane visual complement to his often intense musical output.

However, Boone's artistry is not without its criticisms and areas for potential growth. His vocal delivery, particularly the intense, raspy, and sometimes "screaming" quality in climactic sections, is polarizing, drawing both admiration for its raw emotion and critique for perceived "shrillness" or irritation. There are also accusations of repetitive song structures, with some listeners finding that many of his album tracks follow a similar dynamic build, potentially leading to a sense of predictability. While his lyrics effectively convey immediate emotional pleas, some critics and listeners desire greater depth or reflection beyond these initial outpourings. Navigating the "CCM" label will also be a factor in his artistic development, especially if his primary aim remains a broader "pop soul" identity.

The very aspects of Boone's vocal delivery that draw criticism—its intensity, liberal use of rasp, and the full-throated "scream"—are likely integral to what makes him stand out and connect so deeply with his fanbase. This distinctive, if divisive, signature is a powerful tool for conveying raw emotion. It is this potent emotional and vocal formula, particularly evident in "Beautiful Things" , that propelled the song to massive success. However, the observation that his album songs tend to follow a similar dynamic structure presents a crucial challenge for his long-term artistic growth. True artistic authenticity often necessitates evolution, which might require consciously breaking from a previously successful formula to explore new musical and structural territories. This "authenticity vs. formula" dilemma will be key to his longevity.

Boone's trajectory also highlights the "double-edged sword" of a viral signature sound in the modern music industry. A distinctive vocal style or song structure, like his dynamic shifts from soft verses to powerful, raspy choruses, can fuel explosive viral success on platforms like TikTok. Yet, this same signature, if not complemented by broader artistic versatility in subsequent work, can quickly become overexposed or perceived as a gimmick, potentially hindering perceptions of depth or range. This is a common challenge for artists who achieve breakthrough via viral hits.

Looking ahead, Benson Boone has the potential to be a significant and lasting voice for his generation if he can maintain his perceived authenticity while continuing to evolve artistically. The primary challenge will be to avoid being pigeonholed as a "one-hit wonder" or becoming stale if he relies too heavily on a singular, albeit effective, formula. His themes of anxiety, the profound fear of loss, and the search for stable love and meaning resonate powerfully, particularly with a younger demographic navigating similar complex emotional landscapes. His willingness to express these raw emotions with such unbridled intensity positions him as an emotional conduit for many of his listeners. The success of "Beautiful Things" has undeniably made a mark; his future impact will depend on his capacity for artistic development, diversification, and the continued ability to articulate the emotional undercurrents of his audience in compelling ways.
