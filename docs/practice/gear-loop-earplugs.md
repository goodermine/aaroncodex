# Gear — Loop Experience 2 earplugs

**What they are.** Reusable filtered earplugs (the *Tomorrowland × Loop
Experience 2* colourway). Unlike foam plugs, they lower the whole sound level
fairly **evenly across frequencies** rather than just killing the highs — so
music (and your own voice) stays natural, just quieter.

| Spec | Value |
|---|---|
| Attenuation | **17 dB SNR** (12 dB NRR) |
| Filter character | Even/low-colouration — keeps sound "crisp," music-focused |
| Fit | 4 silicone tip sizes (XS–L); seal per ear matters |
| Louder-night option | *Experience 2 Plus* adds the **Loop Mute** insert for extra reduction on demand |

**Owned as of:** August 2026 — delivered, in testing.

## Why Aaron bought them — hearing protection

Aaron sings/watches at loud karaoke rooms; the Brighton Hotel measures around
**105 dB** just standing there (see `memory/006-brisbane-venues-timezone.md`).
At 105 dB, safe unprotected exposure is only minutes. Knocking ~15–17 dB off
brings that to roughly **88–90 dB**, where safe exposure is hours — a large cut
in dose (every 3 dB roughly halves the sound energy). This alone justifies them.

> Real-world caveat: the 17 dB is a lab figure with a perfect seal. Work the tip
> sizes until each ear gives a clear "sealed" drop; an uneven seal skews the
> pitch reference between ears.

## The open question — do they also help the *singing*?

Plausibly yes, and for a measured reason. Your own voice reaches your ears partly
through **bone conduction**; normally that leaks back out the open ear canal.
Plug the canal and it gets trapped — the **occlusion effect** — so with plugs in,
your own voice becomes *more* prominent while the band gets quieter. Your
voice-to-background ratio improves in the direction you want.

That lands on a known weak point on stage. The Brighton *Kung Fu Fighting* take
note reads "backing music was unusually loud and made self-monitoring
difficult," and the measured stage habit when you can't hear yourself is to
**push** — loud gear early, deeper scoops, support abandoned for volume. Anything
that lets you hear yourself against a loud band directly counters that, and pairs
with the campaign's one on-stage cue: *one gear in reserve — the mic does the
loudness* (`docs/handoffs/COMPETITION_CAMPAIGN_DEC_2026.md`).

### Caveats before relying on them
- **Occlusion changes your self-sound** — your voice feels boomier and "inside
  your head." It won't make you sing flat, but it's a different reference, so
  there's an adjustment period. Don't debut them cold on a competition stage;
  wear them at Friday karaoke first.
- **One-ear option** — some singers prefer a single plug in the ear facing the
  speakers, protection where the blast is, natural hearing on the other side.
- Relates to `memory/008-headphone-discomfort.md` (you sing worse with closed
  headphones at the stand) — earplugs are a lighter, different approach to the
  same monitoring problem.

## The measured test (open)

To turn "feels like it helps" into evidence, record the same song twice at the
same loud level — **plugs out** (Take A) then **plugs in** (Take B) — and compare
the raw onset measures. The prediction: if hearing yourself better stops you
sliding up to find notes, **clean-entry % rises and scoop depth shrinks** in Take
B. Home studio is the ideal place to run it (clean capture → trustworthy
numbers).
