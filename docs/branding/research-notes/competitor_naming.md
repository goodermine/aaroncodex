# Competitor naming and naming best practice for "VOX Analysis" (vocal performance analysis suite)

Scope note: research done 14 Oct 2026 with about 18 search/fetch calls. Much of the 2026 "best AI vocal coach" coverage is published by competitors (Singing Carrots, Bloom Vocal), so treat it as their marketing claims, not independent testing. I have not proposed final names; open naming territories are flagged as inferences.

## 1. Competitor names, naming styles, taglines and positioning

### Takeaway
The consumer category is dominated by **descriptive and compound "Sing-/Vocal-/Voice-" names** (SingSharp, SingTrue, Simply Sing, Vocal Pitch Monitor, VocalTuner, Singing Coach AI, Rate My Singing). A minority use **playful/evocative** names (Singing Carrots, Bloom Vocal, Smule, Yousician). The scientific tools use **Latin/Italian or plainly technical** names (VoceVista, Overtone Analyzer, Praat, Sonic Visualiser). The names people remember and that sold or grew are mostly distinctive coined or arbitrary ones (Smule, Yousician, Melodyne, Auto-Tune, iZotope), not "Vocal + noun" names.

### Cited findings

**Consumer / AI vocal-coach apps (2025–2026)**
- **Singing Carrots**: playful/arbitrary name. Site tagline: "Your AI Vocal Coach". Also seen as "Get a personal vocal coach, powered by AI". Web app, iPhone app since July 2026. Measures cents-level tuning, pitch stability, per-note tracking and progress. From $119.99/year — [Singing Carrots blog, updated 14 Oct 2026](https://singingcarrots.com/blog/top-7-ai-vocal-coaches/); [Singwell](https://singwell.eu/singing-apps/)
- **Yousician**: coined portmanteau (you + musician). Positioned as gamified, structured music education across instruments, with singing as one module. Premium from $7.49/month — [Singing Carrots blog](https://singingcarrots.com/blog/top-7-ai-vocal-coaches/). The company also runs a separate tuner brand, **GuitarTuna** (descriptive compound) — [Wikipedia: Yousician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yousician)
- **SingSharp**: descriptive/suggestive compound (sing + "sharp", a musical pun). Positioned as an AI vocal coach that detects breath alongside pitch, with a sub-feature called "Voice Mentor AI" — [Singing Carrots blog](https://singingcarrots.com/blog/top-7-ai-vocal-coaches/)
- **Smule**: coined name. It launched in 2012 as "Sing! Karaoke" / "Sing!" and the app was later renamed to the company name "Smule". It is positioned as social karaoke ("more performance than training") with AI voice effects and pitch correction — [Wikipedia: Smule (app)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smule_(app)); [TechCrunch 2012](https://techcrunch.com/2012/08/08/smule-social-sing/); [Singing Carrots blog](https://singingcarrots.com/blog/top-7-ai-vocal-coaches/)
- **Bloom Vocal**: evocative word plus descriptor. Tagline: "Smarter vocal practice with AI coaching", focused on K-pop and pop. Scores five rubric categories (breath support, pitch accuracy, tone & register, rhythmic stability, expression). Its tools are named descriptively (pitch trainer, breath meter, range tester, vibrato analyzer, timbre analyzer). Bloom Plus costs $9.99/month — [Bloom Vocal guide, updated 29 Jul 2026](https://www.bloomvocal.site/en/blog/best-ai-vocal-coach-apps-2026)
- **Simply Sing**: master brand plus a descriptive verb. It is "song-first learning from the Simply Piano makers" — [Singing Carrots blog](https://singingcarrots.com/blog/top-7-ai-vocal-coaches/). The company **JoyTunes rebranded as "Simply" in July 2022** so the company name matched its product line (Simply Piano, Simply Guitar, Simply Sing, Simply Draw) — [Wikipedia: Simply](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simply_(software_company))
- **Singing Coach AI**: purely descriptive. It gives feedback on pitch, breath, vibrato and timing, with a conversational coach. User reviews flag inconsistent AI — [Bloom Vocal review](https://www.bloomvocal.site/en/blog/singing-coach-ai-review-2026); [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.singingcoach.ai&hl=en_IN)
- **Newer entrants named in the Oct 2026 update**:
  - **Vocalista**: Italian-flavoured "vocal" word. iPhone only, with AI feedback on breathiness and resonance.
  - **Time to Sing**: descriptive phrase, from a small Berlin team.
  - **Rate My Singing**: descriptive phrase, from LindaleAI. It gives 0–100 scores for pitch, tone, breath, vibrato and expression.
  - Source: [Singing Carrots blog](https://singingcarrots.com/blog/top-7-ai-vocal-coaches/)
- Bloom Vocal also lists **Sing & See**, **Simply Sing**, **Vocalista** and **Singeo** as "newer entrants", without launch dates — [Bloom Vocal guide](https://www.bloomvocal.site/en/blog/best-ai-vocal-coach-apps-2026)
- **VoCo Vocal Coach**: coined abbreviation plus descriptor. Free exercises; last updated in 2021 — [Singing Carrots blog](https://singingcarrots.com/blog/top-7-ai-vocal-coaches/)
- **Warm-up and exercise apps**:
  - **Erol Singer's Studio**: founder name plus descriptor, positioned on posture.
  - **Vocalizer**: descriptive agent noun.
  - **Swiftscales**: alliterative compound, a scale and routine builder.
  - **Vocal Ease 2**: pun, from Arnold McCuller.
  - **7 Minute Vocal Warmup**: descriptive and benefit-led.
  - Source: [Singwell](https://singwell.eu/singing-apps/)
- **Pitch-monitor apps**: **VocalTuner – Pitch Training**, **PitchVox – Vocal Pitch Monitor**, **Vocal Pitch Monitor – Voice Whiz**, and a Windows app called **Vocal Pitch Monitor**. All are descriptive and nearly interchangeable — [App Store: VocalTuner](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/vocaltuner-pitch-training/id1505735245); [Google Play: PitchVox](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.siwooeo.pitchvox&hl=en_CA); [App Store: Voice Whiz](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/vocal-pitch-monitor-voice-whiz/id1579828873); [Microsoft Store](https://apps.microsoft.com/detail/9ntfh80xsv4t?hl=en-US&gl=US)
- **"Vox" apps that already exist**: **Vox Tools: Learn to Sing**, from Vox Vocal Studio (Speech Level Singing teachers) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/vox-tools-learn-to-sing/id568721281?platform=iphone). **Voxtrain – Learn to Sing** — [voxtrain.com](https://www.voxtrain.com/). **PitchVox** — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.siwooeo.pitchvox&hl=en_CA)

**Scientific / voice-science tools**
- **VoceVista**: Italian/Latin compound ("voice" + "view"). A real-time spectrum analyser showing pitch, harmonics and a vowel chart. The basic package is about $99; a Pro tier (about 399, currency unstated) adds electroglottography (EGG) for "professionals, vocal pedagogues, science". It was formerly called **"EGGs for Singers"** (co-founded 2007), and its voice-focused sibling is **Overtone Analyzer** (descriptive) — [VoceVista intro](https://www.vocevista.com/en/doc/introduction/); [VoceVista about](https://www.vocevista.com/en/about/)

**Pro audio vocal tools (benchmarks for brand strength)**
- **Auto-Tune (Antares)**: suggestive compound that became a household word. Sources describe "every pitch corrector is now commonly called Auto-Tune". In 2009 Antares sent a cease-and-desist that forced an iPhone app called "AutoTuner" to rename. This shows both the value of a strong mark and the cost of defending a near-descriptive one — [Digital Music News 2009](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2009/11/10/autotune/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=21761)
- **Melodyne (Celemony)**: coined blend (melody + dyne/dynamic). Celemony is itself a coined company name (founded 2000, Munich). Sound On Sound (2020, as quoted secondarily) said Melodyne had "joined Auto-Tune in that realm of ubiquity where people use its name as a verb" — [Wikipedia: Celemony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celemony_Software); [Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/auto-tune-vs-melodyne)

### Inferences
- Names that tend to be remembered or cited as strong in this space are distinctive: Smule, Yousician, Melodyne, Auto-Tune, Singing Carrots. Smule even replaced its descriptive original name ("Sing!") with the distinctive company name. The many "Vocal Pitch Monitor" clones show that descriptive names give no differentiation in app stores.
- Every consumer app is positioned as a "coach" or "trainer", and the AI wave has made "AI vocal coach" a generic category label. Rate My Singing (LindaleAI) and Bloom's five-category rubric are the closest to a "score/verdict" positioning. No competitor I found brands itself around **measurement credibility** or a **professional reference benchmark** (e.g. "10 = typical pro"). That appears to be an open positioning lane.
- The scientific tools (VoceVista, Praat, Sonic Visualiser) name themselves as instruments. Consumer apps name themselves as companions or coaches. A name for a "lab-grade measurement plus verdict" product could borrow the instrument register (precision, lab, scope) without the clinical coldness.

### Gaps
- I did not fetch official taglines for Vanido, SingTrue, Singeo, Swiftscales, Tessitura Pro, Voice Tutor or Sing & See. Their positioning is not verified here.
- I found no independent (non-vendor) ranking of which singing-app names are "most memorable". Statements about strength are inferences from adoption and genericisation evidence.
- Praat and Sonic Visualiser naming was not researched in this pass (Praat is Dutch for "talk", from training knowledge, not verified here).

## 2. Saturated vs open vocal and musical terms in names

### Takeaway
**Sing-, Vocal-, Voice-, Pitch- and Vox- are saturated.** "Vox" carries a specific trademark and recognition risk because VOX is a long-standing music-equipment brand (Korg-owned VOX Amplification Ltd), and several vocal apps already use "Vox". Latin and Italian vocal terms (voce, vocalista, canto) are partly used. Technical voice-science terms (formant, timbre, tessitura, overtone) appear mostly in descriptive feature names or niche tools.

### Cited findings
- **Vox**:
  - VOX Amplification Limited holds US "VOX" registrations covering amplifiers for musical apparatus and related audio goods — [Justia: VOX Amplification Ltd marks](https://trademarks.justia.com/owners/vox-amplification-limited-3493176); [Justia VOX 90817520](https://trademarks.justia.com/908/17/vox-90817520.html)
  - Vox has been owned by Korg since 1992. The name is Latin for "voice" — [Wikipedia: Vox (company)](https://www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/Vox_AC15)
  - Existing vocal apps include Vox Tools, Voxtrain and PitchVox — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/vox-tools-learn-to-sing/id568721281?platform=iphone); [Voxtrain](https://www.voxtrain.com/); [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.siwooeo.pitchvox&hl=en_CA)
- **Sing-**: SingSharp, SingTrue, Simply Sing, Singing Carrots, Singing Coach AI, Sing & See, Singeo, Time to Sing, Rate My Singing, Smule's original "Sing!" — [Singing Carrots blog](https://singingcarrots.com/blog/top-7-ai-vocal-coaches/); [Bloom Vocal](https://www.bloomvocal.site/en/blog/best-ai-vocal-coach-apps-2026)
- **Vocal-**: Bloom Vocal, Vocalista, Vocalizer, Vocal Ease, VocalTuner, Vocal Pitch Monitor, 7 Minute Vocal Warmup, VoCo Vocal Coach — sources as above plus [Singwell](https://singwell.eu/singing-apps/)
- **Voce/voice**: VoceVista, Voice Whiz, Voice Mentor AI (a SingSharp feature) — [VoceVista](https://www.vocevista.com/en/about/); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/vocal-pitch-monitor-voice-whiz/id1579828873)
- **Tune/Pitch**: Auto-Tune, VocalTuner, Simply Tune, GuitarTuna, PitchVox, Vocal Pitch Monitor. Antares has actively enforced against "Auto-Tune"-like app names ("AutoTuner") — [Digital Music News](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2009/11/10/autotune/)
- **Overtone**: Overtone Analyzer (VoceVista family) — [VoceVista](https://www.vocevista.com/en/doc/introduction/)
- **Timbre**: used as a feature label ("timbre analyzer" in Bloom Vocal) — [Bloom Vocal](https://www.bloomvocal.site/en/blog/best-ai-vocal-coach-apps-2026)

### Inferences
- **"VOX Analysis" as a trade name is weak on two counts.** "Analysis" is generic. "VOX" is a recognised music-tech mark in adjacent goods (amplifiers, music electronics) and is crowded with vocal apps. If the product will be sold or licensed, a buyer's diligence would likely flag it. A trademark attorney should check class 9 (software) and class 41 (education) before investing in it.
- Terms that look **less claimed** (from these searches, not exhaustive): measurement or instrument metaphors (gauge, lab, scope, benchmark, calibre), "reference/pro-standard" vocabulary, phonetics or voice-science roots other than vox/voce (phon-, formant), and arbitrary real words unrelated to singing. None of these were checked against trademark registers.
- **Tessitura** is already the name of a product (Tessitura Pro, per the brief), and Tessitura is also a well-known arts-CRM software brand (training knowledge, not verified here), so treat it as taken. Aria is widely used across software (not verified in this pass).

### Gaps
- I did not search USPTO, EUIPO or IP Australia registers directly. Any conclusion about "free" terms needs a formal clearance search.
- Usage of canto/cantor, aria, formant and tessitura in app-store names was not checked app by app.
- I did not verify whether Vox Media's marks extend to software or audio classes.

## 3. Branding-expert guidance: length, pronounceability, trademark strength, domains

### Takeaway
The trademark spectrum runs **fanciful (coined) > arbitrary > suggestive > descriptive > generic**. Sources advise founders to aim for fanciful, arbitrary or suggestive names, because descriptive names are weak and need proof of acquired distinctiveness. Practical SaaS guidance favours short, easily spelled and pronounceable names. It also advises matching the domain to the name, and treats .ai as now mainstream for AI-first startups while .com remains dominant.

### Cited findings
- **Spectrum**:
  - Fanciful marks are invented words (Exxon, Rolex) and the most protectable. Arbitrary marks are real words unrelated to the product (Apple for computers). Suggestive marks hint at a quality (Netflix, Coppertone). Descriptive marks need secondary meaning under Lanham Act §2(f). Generic terms are unprotectable — [DRM: spectrum of distinctiveness](https://www.drm.com/articles/trademarks-the-spectrum-of-distinctiveness/); [Mek IP: four types](https://www.mekiplaw.com/the-four-types-of-trademarks/); [Tucker IP](https://www.tuckerup.com/?p=13920)
  - It is "often difficult to predict whether certain wording will be considered suggestive or descriptive". The practical test: if the name answers "what is it?" rather than "who is it?", it leans descriptive — [Trembly Law](https://tremblylaw.com/blog/what-are-some-characteristics-of-a-strong-trademark/); [echai ventures](https://echai.ventures/d2c/d2c-brand-concept/fanciful-vs-descriptive-brand-name)
  - Cooley advises treating a name as a trademark early, to avoid later disputes — [Cooley Fund Lawyer](https://thefundlawyer.cooley.com/trademark-considerations-and-naming-venture-capital-funds/)
- **Domain strategy (vendor sources; self-reported statistics)**:
  - About 95% of 550+ SaaS companies analysed use .com. Only 53% of YC Winter 2024 startups chose .com, and 28% used .ai — [DomainDetails](https://domaindetails.com/kb/getting-started/saas-domain-naming-strategy)
  - .ai suits companies where AI is fundamental. Starting on an alternative TLD is acceptable if you plan to buy the .com later. Check name and domain availability together — [DomainDetails](https://domaindetails.com/kb/getting-started/saas-domain-naming-strategy); [Spaceship](https://www.spaceship.com/blog/saas-product-name-guide/)
- **Name qualities**:
  - Short and simple; memorable; clearly spelled; phonetically pronounceable.
  - Shouldn't lock you into one product.
  - Avoid hyphens and numbers.
  - Keep domains under about 12 characters.
  - Secure the trademark early.
  - Expect domain scarcity and compromise.
  - Sources: [Spaceship](https://www.spaceship.com/blog/saas-product-name-guide/); [SuperOps](https://superops.com/blog/general/lessons-in-naming-your-saas-startup); [DEV: naming a SaaS in 2026](https://dev.to/eddie_dev_og/how-to-actually-name-a-saas-startup-in-2026-a-practical-40-minute-method-29fc)
- **Real-world enforcement risk for near-descriptive names**: Antares's C&D forced "AutoTuner" to rename because it was "nearly identical" to AUTO-TUNE® and "directly competitive". The small developer could not afford to fight — [Digital Music News](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2009/11/10/autotune/)

### Inferences
- For a product that may be sold or licensed, a **coined or arbitrary master name is the asset a buyer pays for**. Descriptive feature words (analysis, coach, monitor, pitch) can sit underneath as product descriptors without carrying the trademark weight.
- An Australian developer selling internationally should test pronunciation across English accents plus the major markets of the competitors (Finland/EU, Israel, US, Korea, given Bloom's K-pop focus). Two-syllable names with open vowels (Smule, Melodyne, Yousician) travel well. This is an inference, not a cited rule.
- "get[name].com", "[name].ai" and "[name].app" are common fallbacks. The guidance is that the name must still match the domain exactly enough to be typed from hearing it.

### Gaps
- I found no evidence-based source specific to the .app TLD.
- I found no music- or edtech-specific naming-agency study. The guidance above is general SaaS/startup material, mostly from vendor or law-firm marketing pages.
- No source quantified the effect of name length on app-store conversion.

## 4. Brand architecture for multi-product audio suites

### Takeaway
Successful vocal and audio software companies mostly use a **"house of brands" or endorsed model**: a distinctive company brand with distinctive product names (iZotope: Ozone, Nectar, RX, Neutron; Celemony: Melodyne; Antares: Auto-Tune). Consumer edtech players instead use a **branded house** with descriptive suffixes (Simply Piano/Guitar/Sing). The product names, not the corporate umbrella, were what kept value when groups were restructured.

### Cited findings
- **iZotope** sells distinct named products: RX, Ozone, Neutron, Nectar and the rest of the range. Its latest release was RX 12, reviewed in April 2026 — [MusicTech](https://musictech.com/news/industry/izotope-joins-boris-fx-exits-native-instruments/)
- **Soundwide → Native Instruments** (2022–23): the umbrella name "Soundwide" (formed after acquiring Brainworx, Plugin Alliance and Sound Stacks) was dropped in June 2023 for "Native Instruments" because NI was "a brand that more people know and love" and Soundwide was less recognised. iZotope, Brainworx and Plugin Alliance kept their own brands. Integration was handled through one login and one installer (Native Access) — [RouteNote](https://routenote.com/blog/izotope-brainworx-plugin-alliance-join-native-instruments/); [Mix](https://www.mixonline.com/business/native-instruments-absorbs-izotope-brainworx-plugin-alliance); [MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/news/izotope-brainworx-plugin-alliance-native-instruments&quot)
- **Simply (formerly JoyTunes)**: the company renamed itself in July 2022 to match its descriptive product family (Simply Piano, Guitar, Sing, Tune, Draw), "to better reflect its broader focus on creative hobbies" — [Wikipedia: Simply](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simply_(software_company))
- **Yousician** runs the main Yousician service plus a separately branded tuner app, GuitarTuna — [Wikipedia: Yousician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yousician)
- **Muse Group** (formed April 2021) is an umbrella over well-known brands that kept their names: Ultimate Guitar, MuseScore, Tonebridge, Audacity (2021), StaffPad (2021) and Hal Leonard (2023) — [Scoring Notes](https://www.scoringnotes.com/news/muse-group-formed-to-support-musescore-ultimate-guitar-acquires-audacity/); [MusicTech](https://musictech.com/news/audacity-acquired-muse-group-ultimate-guitar-musescore/); [Wikipedia: Muse Group](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Muse_Group)
- **VoceVista** keeps a parallel product, Overtone Analyzer, and sells tiered editions (basic/Pro) under one name — [VoceVista](https://www.vocevista.com/en/doc/introduction/)

### Inferences
- For a suite with an **analysis product, a polishing product and a real-time pitch monitor** (currently "Analyze / Polish / Fused + /monitor" in the voxsuite repo), two common patterns fit:
  - (a) **Branded house**: Master + plain descriptors, e.g. "[Master] Analyze", "[Master] Polish", "[Master] Monitor", as with Simply. It is cheapest to build and clearest for consumers, and all value accrues to one sellable mark.
  - (b) **Endorsed brands**: distinct product names "by [Master]", as with iZotope's Ozone/Nectar/RX. This is costlier, but each product could be licensed or sold separately.
- For a solo developer, (a) looks more practical at launch. The evidence from NI/iZotope/Muse suggests that buyers value distinctive, separately recognisable product marks. So the master name itself should be distinctive (fanciful or arbitrary) rather than "VOX Analysis".
- Sub-product descriptors such as Analyze, Polish and Monitor are generic and should not be relied on as trademarks.

### Gaps
- I did not find Waves' naming rationale. Its product naming (e.g. CLA-, H-, Renaissance series) was not researched in this pass.
- I found no published study quantifying the value of branded-house versus house-of-brands models at exit in music software.

## 5. Acquisition lessons: brand value at sale

### Takeaway
In music tech, acquirers keep **strong, distinct product brands** intact, and drop or swap **weak umbrella names**. Recent examples: iZotope sold on to Boris FX intact in 2026; Muse Group kept every brand; Soundwide was abandoned. Product brand equity, plus an installed base of active licences and subscriptions, is what transfers at sale.

### Cited findings
- **NI/iZotope chain**:
  - 2021: iZotope and Native Instruments joined under Francisco Partners.
  - 2022: the group became "Soundwide".
  - June 2023: renamed to Native Instruments.
  - January 2026: NI entered preliminary insolvency.
  - May 2026: Francisco Partners sold the NI brand (then including iZotope, Brainworx and Plugin Alliance) to inMusic.
  - 2 July 2026: iZotope was reported as acquired by Boris FX (VFX software), leaving the NI family.
  - Boris FX's CEO cited iZotope as "known for its smart and practical use of AI". iZotope said licences "remain fully active", and no product renames were announced.
  - Sources: [MusicTech](https://musictech.com/news/industry/izotope-joins-boris-fx-exits-native-instruments/). Only one outlet was checked for the 2026 events.
- **Muse Group** acquired Audacity with undisclosed terms. It kept Audacity open source and under its own name, and reported no major structural changes to portfolio companies — [MusicTech](https://musictech.com/news/audacity-acquired-muse-group-ultimate-guitar-musescore/); [CDM](https://cdm.link/audacity-finds-home-along-open-source-musescore-in-new-acquisition-by-growing-muse-group/)
- **Smule** retired its descriptive launch name "Sing! Karaoke" in favour of the distinctive company name, Smule. No date or rationale was found — [Wikipedia: Smule (app)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smule_(app))
- **Yousician**: the latest financing found was a $28M Series B in 2021 (total $35M), with investors including the Amazon Alexa Fund and Paul McCartney's MPL Ventures — [Music Business Worldwide](https://musicbusinessworldwide.com/yousician-raises-28m-in-b-round-new-investors-include-amazon-alexa-fund-and-paul-mccartneys-mpl-ventures); [EU-Startups](https://www.eu-startups.com/2021/04/helsinki-based-yousician-lands-e23-1-million-to-reimagine-music-education/)
- **Antares** defended AUTO-TUNE® even as the word became generic in everyday speech. Sources describe the tension, but no genericide ruling was found — [Digital Music News](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2009/11/10/autotune/); [Grammarist](https://grammarist.com/?p=24543)

### Inferences
- If a sale or licence is a goal, the brand asset should be a **registrable, distinctive mark the owner clearly owns** in the key markets (AU, US, EU). Acquirers inherit and keep such marks. A name that conflicts with an existing music-gear mark such as VOX would be a diligence liability rather than an asset.
- Genericisation (Auto-Tune, Melodyne used as verbs) is a sign of category leadership, but it raises enforcement costs. This is a reason to pair a distinctive mark with a separate generic descriptor (e.g. "[Mark] vocal analysis").

### Gaps
- I could not confirm any Yousician acquisition of another company, or any 2025–2026 acquisition involving Antares, Celemony, Smule or a singing app. Searches returned nothing.
- Deal values for iZotope→Boris FX, NI→inMusic and Muse/Audacity were not disclosed in the sources found, so brand value cannot be quantified.
- Smule's rename date and rationale were not found.
