Since 15 Oct 2026 Aaron sometimes sings with **one in-ear monitor**: a Shure
in-ear plugged (3.5 mm) into the Insta360 lapel-mic receiver, worn in the right
ear, with the other ear open. He hears himself clearly with it. When analysing
or comparing a take, ask or check whether he wore it, and record that in the
take's `take_context.note`, so monitor and no-monitor takes aren't compared
blindly.

Results so far (all 15 Oct 2026, v5 engine):
- My Girl: home take 1 no monitor 8.2, home take 2 one ear 8.5, Bramble live
  one ear 9.4 capture-fair (his best My Girl).
- The Wanderer: home take 1 no monitor 8.8, home take 2 one ear 8.8 (cleaner note
  starts, steadier held notes), Bramble live **no monitor** 9.6 capture-fair.
- Let's Stay Together with BOTH ears in: 7.1 → 6.8, pitch worse. One ear only.
- Caveat: each home monitored take was also the second run of that song. So
  far the monitor helps but isn't required.

At gigs, the host may not realise he has his own monitor and keep checking the
front wedge. Tip for Aaron: tell the host before starting that he's on his own
in-ear and the wedge can stay as is.

> evidence: "using the Insta360 mic connected to the transmitter… Shure actual proper ear monitor plugged into it with a 3.5 jack. And I've just got that one ear, and it's brilliant." — session of 2026-10-15
> also: "I wore the ear monitor for the very first track my girl… I used it for the first one not for the second one" — session of 2026-10-15
